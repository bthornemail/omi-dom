omi-imo / agreement

Agreement is the closest thing to truth.

Three peers. No server, no clock, no authority, no merge function. One XOR says how far apart they are and exactly which bytes. One more brings them back — because the distance and the displacement are the same object.

declarative syntax

The protocol declares arrangements, not meanings. It does not decide what a number is and it does not decide who was right.

what this is, precisely

With two peers you learn the magnitude of a disagreement. With three you also learn which peer is the odd one out, from the same single XOR: if two agree and one does not, then a ^ b ^ c is the outlier's value. Alice 5, Bob 5, Carol 9 gives 5 ^ 5 ^ 9 = 9 — which is Carol. No vote, no quorum, nothing to elect.

It is not a clock. XOR is commutative, so a ^ b == b ^ a — it measures the magnitude of a difference and never the order of two concurrent edits. It does not replace vector clocks and cannot tell you which edit came first. The narrow claim is the true one: clocks exist to order events, and if all you need is how far apart states are and how to undo the difference, you need no clock at all.

It refuses rather than guesses. Three-way disagreement has no majority, so the portal says so and does not pick a winner. The XOR of three values is also not the agreement test — three copies of x fold to x, not to zero, because three is odd. Agreement is measured pairwise.

Tamper evidence comes from the representation: a peer's state is one word, and attested is the last state that came through its own atomic gate. A quiet rewrite moves the state and cannot move the attestation. Not a security primitive — a free consequence.

a ^ a == 0 for any a, anywhere, in any base. That is a logical truth rather than a measurement, which is why it is the only genuinely invariant thing here.
